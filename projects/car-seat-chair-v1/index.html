Car seat computer chair (V1)

A real car seat converted into a desk chair, with its powered adjustment working and a custom power system that switches between mains and battery automatically.

Car seats are some of the most comfortable, adjustable seats ever made, and they get scrapped in their thousands. I took a 2019 Kia Stinger GT seat with powered adjustment and a built-in fan and turned it into my everyday computer chair, keeping all of its electronics working.

The interesting problem was power. A car seat expects a 12 V supply that can deliver a lot of current briefly: the fan draws a couple of amps continuously, but the adjustment motors pull around 12.5–15 A when they move. I designed a power system around a 19 V supply feeding two paths:

  • a buck converter stepping down to a 12 V rail for the seat
  • a charger module (HW-845) keeping an onboard battery topped up

A dual-Schottky diode OR-ing circuit combines the mains-derived rail and the battery. When the chair is plugged in, it runs from mains; unplug it and it fails over to battery seamlessly, with no switch and no interruption. So the chair can move around the room without trailing cables and still adjust.

V1 is fully functional and in daily use. It's built for function first, so the finish is utilitarian. A V2 with a more feature-rich seat and proper aesthetic work is on the list.

Built with

  • 2019 Kia Stinger GT seat
  • 19 V PSU
  • HW-845 charger
  • buck converter
  • dual-Schottky OR-ing failover
  • small 12 V lead-acid battery
  • heavy-duty castors and star base
  • short heavy-duty hydraulic cylinder
  • heavy-duty office chair plate
  • 10 mm mild steel adapter plate to the seat rails
