Giant Spiritomb build

A life-size, fully lit model of Spiritomb, with an animated holographic face that appears to float inside the stone.

I wanted to see whether I could take a creature from a game and make it feel physically present in a room, rather than just a static figure on a shelf. The result stands about 60cm tall and is built from 7 individually printed parts, with no sanding, priming or painting: the look of aged, cracked stone comes entirely from the filament finish.

The centrepiece is the face. Instead of a fixed sculpted expression, it's displayed as hologram-style imagery that seems to hover in the void at the centre of the swirl. The device uses a technology called Persistence of Vision (POV) to create the illusion of a floating 3D holographic image. It consists of rapidly rotating arms fitted with tiny RGB LEDs, which are switched on and off in precise synchronisation with the rotation of the device. By illuminating individual LEDs at specific positions during each rotation, the device effectively draws an image in mid-air. The rapid movement and flashing of the LEDs are interpreted by the human eye as a continuous image, while the absence of a physical screen means that only the illuminated parts are visible, making the image appear to float in space. Although marketed as a hologram projector, it is technically a rotating LED display rather than a true holographic projection. It's surrounded by internal lighting that shines through the transparent purple parts and makes them look alive, so the whole thing glows from within in a dark room.

The hardest part by far was the 3D modelling. I was inspired by a similar project, but ultimately started everything from scratch to do things the way I wanted to. The challenge was balancing parts that are easy to print against parts that are easy to assemble, without compromising the aesthetics. The electronics, by contrast, were easy to hide in all that empty space inside. The glow comes from 3 super bright LED pucks, which generated so much heat that I had to create a heat sink for them.

Built with

Bambu Lab A1 with grey matte PLA and purple translucent PLA · Fusion 360 · 224-LED hologram fan · 3 LED pucks with a custom heat sink · garage door wireless button used to turn it on and off remotely · filament finish, no painting
